Diffusion forcing: why MIRA can run for hours without drifting

A world model predicts one frame, then feeds that prediction back in as context for the next one, forever. Tiny errors compound. MIRA was trained on 4-second clips, yet it rolls out for hours. The ingredient that makes the difference is how the context is treated during training. In this chapter you'll watch two models that differ only in that one choice play the same match side by side.

1 · The trap: training on truth, running on your own guesses

Teacher forcing (used by earlier diffusion world models such as DIAMOND and GameNGen): at training time the past frames are always the real frames. Only the next frame is noised and denoised.

At rollout time, though, the past frames are the model's own slightly-wrong outputs. The model has never seen such inputs. It reacts badly, which makes the next frame more wrong, and so on. This is exposure bias.

Diffusion forcing (Chen et al., 2024): every frame in the training clip gets its own random noise level τ. The model denoises all of them at once, and each frame can only look at the (noisy) frames before it. So the model constantly practises predicting from imperfect context and learns how much to trust it.

2 · Same match, three worlds

Below, a real physics engine ("Rocket Pong", our stand-in for Rocket League) runs next to two learned world models. The models are small networks that predict the next state from the last 4 states and both players' actions. They have the same architecture, data (600 bot matches) and training steps. The only difference is whether their context was clean (teacher forcing) or independently noised per frame (diffusion forcing). All three receive the exact same action stream. 3 · The knob MIRA turns at inference: context noise

Because diffusion forcing trained the model on noisy context, you can deliberately re-noise the past frames during rollout. That tells the model "don't trust the past blindly". The paper evaluates with past-context noise 0.2 and measures what happens from 0 to 0.5 (Fig. 10):

  • With MIRA's frozen-DINOv3 codec, quality is flat across noise levels. The latent space is so well-behaved that clean context already works.
  • With the more fragile "DINO-distilled" codec, clean context collapses (gFID 13.6 → 81.7 over 12 s), and a little noise rescues it.

Try the context noise slider above. Our toy is like MIRA's frozen-DINO codec: it is already stable with clean context (0), so re-noising isn't needed. It does have a cost here. The toy's state is tiny and exact (positions and velocities), so the injected noise leaks into the predicted motion and blurs how precisely the cars respond to keys (you'll measure that in Ch. 07).

Role of diffusion forcing in one sentence It turns "predict the next frame from perfect history" into "predict the next frame from whatever history you have". Together with the smooth DINO latent space from Chapter 01, that's what keeps MIRA stable far beyond its 4-second training window.
