MIRA, explained

MIRA is a 5-billion-parameter world model that is a game of Rocket League. It has no physics or rendering engine: it predicts four players' video, 20 frames per second, from their keyboard presses. These chapters take it apart one component at a time, with demos that actually run in your browser: a real DINOv3 encoder, flow-matching models you train yourself, and learned toy world models you can play inside.

MIRA's pipeline

shapes from the paper
The one-line summary Encode each player's view into a small DINO-based latent. Let a transformer, steered by all four players' keys, generate the next latent with a few flow-matching steps. Decode it into 2 frames per view. Append it to the context and repeat, 10 times a second, for hours.
The three choices the paper says matter most ① predict in the latent of a frozen pretrained encoder (stability), ② diffusion forcing + few-step distillation (long horizons at speed), ③ condition on all four action streams (less uncertainty, consistent views).
