Chapter 06 · Section 5 of the paper

Streaming at 20 fps on one GPU

An interactive world model has a hard deadline: a new frame every 50 ms, whatever the player does. MIRA meets it on a single Nvidia B200. One step (world model + decoder) takes about 70 ms and yields two frames, so roughly 35 ms per frame. This chapter is a working simulator of that serving loop. It plays real MIRA frames, so when the pipeline falls behind, you'll see the stutter.

1 · The rolling context window

KV cache + fixed window of 20 latents

exact window size · real frames

A session starts with prefill: a short clip of real gameplay is encoded and its keys/values are written into the cache. Then each decode step appends one fresh noise latent, denoises it into the next generated latent (conditioned on the newest keys), and drops the oldest. Because past keys and values are cached, every step costs the same no matter how long you play. The decoder only needs the 3 most recent latents. The price: anything older than 2 s is forgotten (the replay and clock failures in Ch. 07).

Top lanes show what the GPU is doing (world-model passes in amber, decoding in blue). The middle lane shows frames waiting in the queue. The bottom lane shows the display reading a frame every 50 ms: green means a new frame, red means it had to repeat the old one (a visible stutter). The paper's design: each step bursts out two frames, a separate sender drains the queue at a steady cadence, and a full queue blocks the producer (backpressure), so generation is paced to the display and never races ahead. Notice the trade-off in the latency box: a deeper queue absorbs more jitter but adds up to 50 ms of delay per buffered frame between pressing a key and seeing its effect.
